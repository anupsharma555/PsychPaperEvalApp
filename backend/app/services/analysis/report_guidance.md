# PaperEval Report Guidance

## General
- Prefer specific, anchored details over generic summaries.
- Keep the report explanatory and detailed: each section should say what matters, why it matters, and the concrete evidence supporting it.
- Preserve exact numbers, sample sizes, instruments, outcomes, comparisons, confidence intervals, p values, and effect directions when available.
- Define or briefly explain study-specific terms, acronyms, interventions, instruments, datasets, or named methods the first time they are central to understanding the paper.
- Integrate term explanations into the relevant section rather than as a glossary. For example, if a paper studies Flip-It, the Introduction should briefly explain what Flip-It is before summarizing the study aim.
- Do not move methods details into Results, result claims into Methods, or interpretation into Results.
- Do not state that information is missing unless the paper explicitly says so.
- If evidence is sparse, write a narrower grounded statement instead of filler.

## Paper Type
- First infer the paper type from the evidence: randomized trial, observational study, qualitative study, review/systematic review/meta-analysis, methods/tool paper, laboratory/preclinical study, case report/series, commentary/editorial, or data brief/report.
- Adjust section expectations to the inferred paper type.
- For review papers, emphasize search strategy, databases, date range, inclusion/exclusion criteria, screening/extraction process, synthesis method, risk-of-bias/quality appraisal, evidence patterns, heterogeneity, and limitations.
- For trials, emphasize design, allocation, blinding, sample, intervention/control, outcomes, follow-up, statistical model, primary and secondary results, adverse events, and limitations.
- For observational studies, emphasize cohort/source population, exposure, outcomes, confounding control, model specification, missing data, sensitivity analyses, associations, and causal caution.
- For laboratory/preclinical studies, emphasize biological system, constructs/reagents, assays, validation experiments, controls, quantification, and functional results.
- For methods/tool papers, emphasize the problem the tool solves, inputs/outputs, validation approach, benchmark/comparator, performance, usability, and limits.
- For data briefs/reports, emphasize data source, survey/sample, measure definitions, weighting/statistics, subgroup comparisons, and population-level implications.

## Introduction
- Include the research question or scientific/clinical problem, prior knowledge gap, study rationale, and objective/hypothesis.
- Explain central terms or interventions that a domain reader needs to interpret the paper.
- Avoid detailed protocols or results unless needed to define the aim.

## Methods
- Include study design, setting/data source, sample/cohort, inclusion/exclusion criteria, intervention/exposure/comparator, outcomes, instruments/measures, timing, statistical models, covariates, missing-data handling, and sensitivity analyses.
- For reviews, include databases, search dates, eligibility criteria, screening, extraction, synthesis, and risk-of-bias method.
- For lab studies, include cell/animal/model system, constructs, assays, validation, controls, quantification, and sequencing/imaging methods.

## Results
- Include primary and secondary outcomes with direction, magnitude, and uncertainty when available.
- Integrate figure and table findings with their labels when they add concrete information.
- Include subgroup, sensitivity, supplemental, or validation findings when central.
- Avoid protocol language and unsupported causal interpretation.

## Discussion
- Include the authors' interpretation, relation to prior literature, proposed mechanisms, strengths, limitations, implications, and generalizability.
- Distinguish interpretation from observed results.

## Conclusion
- Include the main take-home conclusion, practical/research implications, and any explicit caution.
- Do not introduce new evidence not already supported by the paper.
